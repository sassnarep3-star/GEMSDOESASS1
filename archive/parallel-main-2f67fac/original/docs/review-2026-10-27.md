### Official competition facts (checked against the official problem-description page)

The official [DOE GEMS problem description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) says the target is geological faults indicative of geothermal resources; it describes the known-fault labels as incomplete and potentially inaccurate. It identifies USGS and INGENIOUS fault labels, GeoDAWN geophysical products, EPSG:32611 at 100 m for `training_features.tif`, and a distance-weighted Tversky metric with `alpha=0.2`, `beta=0.8`, and a 300 m triangular kernel. It requires a single-band float32 GeoTIFF, values [0,1], matching training-data projection/resolution/bounds, with null/NaN outside the bounds. The provided sample submission is recommended as a template.

The official feature descriptions include surface conductivity and depth to conductive base; detrended elevation and slope; dilatation, shear strain and strain-rate second invariant; isostatic gravity anomaly and its slope; reduced-to-pole magnetic anomaly, total magnetic intensity and TMI derivatives; top-of-crustal magnetic-source depth; and earthquake density. The official page also describes GeoDAWN as magnetic and radiometric surveys, but the available text does not establish that specific radiometric channels are in the competition feature raster. Verify the actual channel manifest before using them.

## 2. What can and cannot be said about H33-2-B2 / 0.2778

### What the evidence supports

The public GEMSDOE32 owner research note offers a plausible mechanism: a prior broad H19-5 support field was thinned, then predictions close to the visible catalogue were pruned in stages. Its notes describe a nested sequence from approximately 44,090 dots (D2.8) to 40,199 (B=1/solo) to 37,654 (B=2), with the owner-reported score rising from 0.2600 to 0.2708 to 0.2778. Those are **OWNER-REPORTED historical score associations** and owner-authored byte-forensics claims, not organizer-confirmed observations re-run here.

Metric-wise, a sparse prediction can score better when it removes predictions with little marginal hidden-truth credit and retains predictions close to true new faults. With `alpha=0.2` and `beta=0.8`, distance-weighted false negatives carry four times the coefficient of false positives, and the 300 m kernel rewards near misses. A smaller raster can still win if removed pixels were redundant or mainly false positives. Conversely, pruning can hurt if it removes genuine hidden fault coverage. The official metric and incomplete-catalogue framing make this a plausible explanation, not a proven causal account.

### What cannot be concluded

- The 0.2778 is not tied to a file hash by an organizer receipt in this repository; the associated leaderboard/private truth is unavailable locally.
- We cannot attribute the score increase causally to the pruning, estimate the hidden TP/FP/FN contributions, or know which individual dots were credited.
- We cannot responsibly claim a new method will exceed 0.2778, the user-mentioned 0.3195, or 0.4413. No calibrated mapping from local holdout to live/private leaderboard score is available here.
- The 0.3195 and 0.4413 values conflict as “current high”; the owner research note also reports a later manual public observation of 0.4437 and says 0.4413 was a cached prior value. None of those is independently refreshed or organizer-confirmed by this checkout. Check the [official live leaderboard](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/leaderboard/) for a dated observation; a leaderboard view still does not identify this team's result without its receipt.

**Research conclusion:** H33-2-B2 is a useful historical benchmark for sparse, metric-aware emission and catalogue-proximity ablation. It is not a recipe to duplicate. The right next scientific question is whether a genuinely distinct, off-catalogue structural cue improves the prescribed blocked holdout—not whether a changed filename or another small density/pruning adjustment can reproduce its reported leaderboard score.
