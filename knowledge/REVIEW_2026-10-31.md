The scoring rule (forum 11516; asymmetric Tversky α=0.2 β=0.8, 300 m triangular kernel, known
catalogue pixels masked) taxes every unmatched emitted pixel at 0.2 but credits each truth pixel
once, by its single best prediction. A dot on a mapped pixel is ignored; a dot 1–2 px away is full
FP risk. The entire 0.2600 → 0.2778 ladder came from **deleting** low-credit dots, not from new
faults. All 12 files ≥ 0.24 across the nine focus sites are ≥ 97.5 % within 3 px of the 0.2600
raster: one field, one family (`evidence/nine_site_board.json`).

## 2. Can we beat 0.2778? (and 0.3195 / the 0.4413/0.4544 top?)

Only with a **different field** at ~40 k dots whose top pixels carry more kernel credit —
especially in the far-field habitat (the champion's dots sit median 19.6 px = 1.96 km from the
catalogue; the hidden lidar truth lives there). Emission tricks on H19-5 are exhausted
(0.2708/0.2710/0.2707 are three independent attempts converging on the same number).

This session's answer, in two parts:

1. **Instrument honesty.** No local instrument forecasts the live score (holdout↔live Spearman
   −0.081 over 17 registry rasters; the hide-and-recover truth is catalogue-derived). The only
   instruments that beat the family base (HOLDOUT-DTI bar 0.1011) are segment-withheld supervised
   learners. A holdout pass is *necessary, not sufficient*.
2. **A genuinely new field, verified unique.** The H-BSTEP concordance learner (below) ranks
   *different* structures from the openness family — measured: its dots overlap the competitive
   family at only 55–63 % within 3 px (limit 70 %), rank correlation ≤ 0.04 (limit 0.90), where the
   prior supervised lanes collided at 70.2–72.3 % and were logged as duplicates.
