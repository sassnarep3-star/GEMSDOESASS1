**Lattice facts (MEASURED-HERE):** 206,895 dots at 5 px spacing; covers 99.87% of the 5,167,373 footprint pixels within 3 px. The 6,649 uncovered pixels are at the footprint edge (median 1 px from the edge). Against the other 17 registry rasters, random dots score 5–18%, so the 70%/3 px test does discriminate there. **Only the lattice saturates it.**

**Why this matters:** the protocol says a candidate is a duplicate if more than 70% of its dots fall within 3 px of any registry raster. Because the lattice covers nearly every pixel, every dense (~30k–45k dot) file fails no matter what it predicts. The gate therefore cannot distinguish a copy from an original. Any "unique" claim based on it is also meaningless. I did not change the rule, and I did not game it by placing dots only on the 6,649 uncovered edge pixels.
