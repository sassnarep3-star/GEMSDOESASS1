Reading the 0.2778 claim

GEMSDOE32's owner-described h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros is reported at 0.2778, not organizer-confirmed in the reviewed evidence. The owner's account describes nested H19-5 support, 2.8-pixel thinning and catalogue-near pruning. Our own saved-byte comparison verifies support relationships, not this narrative's causal effect or an uploaded receipt.

Why it might help: the official metric rewards predictions near hidden truth but penalizes unsupported predictions. Removing redundant or catalogue-adjacent dots may improve the tradeoff, while also risking missed faults. The official scoring mask excludes known-fault pixels exactly, not a protective buffer (staff clarification). A scientifically distinct method could score higher; no new holdout, calibrated estimate, or receipt here establishes that it does.

Source freshness: GEMSDOE24 changed after the 11:39 UTC baseline. Its later research updates report negative / do-not-submit outcomes for new artifacts; two of those artifacts were independently rechecked against the lattice, but their reported holdouts were not rerun and they are not in the frozen historical table.

The nine historical masks were compared directionally at ≤3 Euclidean pixels. All 72 ordered pairs trigger the stipulated >70% stop. This is a retrospective comparison of nine pinned files, not coverage of the full, changing registry.

Shared tools need upstream fixes

The pinned shared evaluator and writer turn every finite positive input into a binary mask, so they cannot preserve continuous probabilities. This is already reported in upstream issue 37. The holdout source also matches the hash in the open report about omitted false-positive mass in empty-truth cells. We independently reproduced issue 42: the surface-stage gate can pass a synthetic preview with 100% dot overlap. These are reasons not to trust an unreviewed shared run. This account has read-only access to the template; no upstream fix or private fork was made.

Four prospective hypotheses

These were ranked before model work. They are not an assigned method lane, results, or proven novel across every project. Expected benefit and cost are qualitative; named mimics must be tested.

1 Cross-strike signed step agreement MED–HIGH COST

Layers: rtp, iso_grav_anom, det_elev. Orient from a local gravity gradient, then test magnetic/topographic step polarity and persistence across strike. Concealed displacement could align these independent contrasts even outside the catalogue. The joint profile interaction differs from the inspected template's generic cross-gradients and ridges; it reuses familiar building blocks. Mimics: lithologic contacts, lava margins and lake terraces. Benefit: possible covered-fault sensitivity; cost: orientation controls, cross-physics alignment and fold-safe tests.

2 Offset magnetic texture MEDIUM COST

Layers: rtp, tmi, depth_to_base_surf. Test whether texture matches after a small bounded along-strike translation across a boundary, stably in adjacent windows. A buried fault may offset magnetic material. This tests correspondence rather than generic gradient alignment. Mimics: volcanic flows, intrusions and remanent magnetization. Benefit: potentially detects buried offsets; cost: matching and negative controls.

3 Gravity–conductivity steps MEDIUM COST

Layers: iso_grav_anom, cond_surf, depth_to_base_surf. Independently estimate step orientations and seek persistent parallel gravity and conductivity transitions at several scales. A fault-bounded basin could contrast in both density and electrical response. Unlike the template's gravity–magnetic cross-gradient, this tests cross-physics step profiles. Mimics: saline playas, stratigraphic contacts, seams and coarse MT resolution. Benefit: possible covered basin boundaries; cost: missingness and resolution controls.

4 Flight-line repeatability HIGH COST · EXTERNAL

Layers: raw GeoDAWN magnetic flight-line and tie-line CSVs and flight paths. Test profile steps at independent crossings before gridding, controlling for direction and terrain clearance. Persistent measurements could reveal structure lost to grid processing; this differs from the inspected gridded-feature approaches. Mimics: levelling artifacts and dikes. Benefit: independent acquisition evidence; cost: large external data and registration. Official metadata list the files, but their bytes and AOI coverage were not verified in this run.
