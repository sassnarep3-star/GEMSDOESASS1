What this session established

Four findings, all measured, none of them the answer we wanted.

01 · The metric

D = α·(TP + FP) + β·K, exactly

Because α + β = 1. Certified to 1e-9 against the shared evaluator and its brute-force oracle. The oracle ceiling is 1.000: predict the truth set exactly and you score 1.0. One unit of recall is worth about 14 units of precision at the champion's operating point.

metric_certification.json ↗

02 · The winner

0.2778 is 0.2600 minus 6,436 dots

The champion is a strict subset of GEMSDOE25's 44,090-dot file: delete everything within 2 px (200 m) of a mapped fault and you get 37,654 dots — recomputed here with symmetric difference 0 against the fetched raster. It is a precision improvement, not a discovery.

03 · The geometry

2.8–3.0 px spacing is already optimal

The calibrated model gives 0.236 at 1.4 px, 0.2778 at 2.8 px, 0.2784 at 3.0 px, 0.272 at 4.0 px. Every dotted submission in this group already sits at the optimum, so re-emitting the champion's field cannot beat it — the field has to change.

04 · The instrument

Our holdout is biased against the winner

The champion's field has exactly zero pixels in common with the public catalogue (minimum distance 1.0 px) — it was built to exclude mapped faults. Our holdout's truth is the catalogue. So a holdout win rewards predicting faults that are already mapped, which is what loses live.

05 · The lever

Precision g is the whole game

Holding the optimal spacing, the model gives DTI 0.278 at g = 0.065 (the champion), 0.454 at g = 0.108 (the leaderboard top) and 0.555 at the g = 0.134 ceiling. Buy precision with a better lineament field — shoreline rejection and buried basement steps are the two cheapest ways.
